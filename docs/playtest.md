# New Eden city playtest (2026-09-23)

The browser path reaches the ending with the labelled fake Jev service: ask Mara a typed power-cell question, record her sourced claim and a part-location assumption, request a Jev lead, pull the workshop lever, collect the cell, collect the shield in the salvage yard, release the depot cage for the relay, install all three at the ship, and launch. Separate browser tests check an unrelated question adds no claim, a late answer after closing dialogue is ignored, and a service outage leaves traversal available until retry. Unit tests cover provenance, contradictory claims, physical access, lead validity, save recovery, and facade collision.

A live Jev browser question, “Where is the power cell?”, selected Mara's authored workshop answer and added her claim. A live ship-computer analysis then selected the offered `power-cell@workshop` lead and cited Mara. The six initial live decisions (four resident activities, one dialogue, one lead) returned in 180–410 ms with no errors. The TypeSafe key was held by the local server. A ten-minute live four-resident city session returned 139 choices with no service or browser errors, but all 137 activity choices were `work`. The repeated behavior prompted a routine constraint so Jev still chooses each activity from a changing valid set. A 60-second post-change live sample returned work, walk, and watch choices while all four residents kept their routines. A blind first-time player session has **not** happened; a fresh player should be given only the start screen and watched for whether they find each route, notice Eli's outdated power-cell rumor, and understand the journal without coaching.

Visual inspection at 1440 × 900 covered arrival, a conversation, the journal, the workshop cell, and the repaired ship. The city reads as a street district with sidewalks, shops, lights, moving cars, pedestrians, and four named residents, but the geometry and human figures are procedural prototype art. The ship model is still abstract and foreground vehicles are noninteractive. Named residents retain which lines they have said and can offer a follow-up response on later encounters; deeper social memory and expressive animation remain future work. No recorded voice performances are present; residents are subtitled.

## Previous wilderness prototype

The seeded browser test completes the functional path: enter the habitat, take an egg from the clutch to expose the trapped regulator, observe a guardian DEFEND response, drop the egg, observe remembered FOLLOW behavior, write a journal hypothesis, collect the drive regulator, install it at the ship, and board to see the ending. The test uses debug teleportation between locations because headless Chromium declines pointer lock; the pickup, drop, Jev decision, journal, repair, and boarding inputs are the same player controls. A separate unit test verifies that the regulator cannot be taken before an egg is moved.

A live Jev encounter with the explorer at the clutch showed two guardians recording the theft, guardians choosing `DEFEND` while the egg was carried, and remembered `FOLLOW` after it was dropped. The full repair-and-escape browser path remains verified with the fake service; the live encounter did not include boarding.

Visual inspection of the ship, nest, repair site, and dusk nest found two problems. A large guardian could overlap the first-person camera, so player movement now keeps a physical distance from creatures. The regulator sat too low behind wreckage, so it has been raised and lit. Review also found that the regulator could be taken without entering the nest; it now sits under the clutch and becomes available when an egg is moved. A guardian can remember a theft only when facing the nearby, visible explorer, and its attack stops once the egg is dropped. The visor shows a bearing and range to the nesting grounds or ship without revealing the guardian rule.

A blind human discovery test has **not** occurred. Give a new player only the start screen and in-game controls, then observe whether they can (1) infer the carried-egg condition from contrasting sightings, (2) recognize that a particular guardian remembers them after the egg is dropped, and (3) find the regulator and complete the repair. Record where they misunderstand calls, posture, journal entries, or the bearing. Do not tell them the rule before asking what they think it is.

The habitat uses locally generated geometry, textures, and audio. It is a complete playable slice but its creature meshes are still prototype art. The browser's software-rendered frame rate is below the target; hardware performance is unverified.
